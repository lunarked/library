Story: Adidas Yeezy (2022 to 2023)
Decision story · Stranded inventory

Adidas was stuck with €1.2 billion of Yeezy shoes and sold every pair

In October 2022, Adidas ended its Yeezy deal with Ye after his antisemitic comments. Millions of finished shoes were left in its warehouses. Adidas could burn them, keep them, or sell them. It chose to sell them in small drops over two years and give part of the profit to charity.

Chapter 1 · The stuck stock

Tuesday, 25 October 2022

Adidas ends its partnership with Ye. It stops making Yeezy shoes and stops paying him. But the shoes already made are still there, in warehouses around the world.

Watch the warehouse. Each box stands for €10 million of shoes.

Oct 2022 · the split

Production stops. The stock is worth about €1.2 billion in sales, or about $1.3 billion. Nobody can sell it while Adidas decides what to do.

Mar 2023 · the warning

Adidas tells investors that if it never sells the shoes, it loses €1.2 billion in sales and takes a €500 million write-off. Together with other costs, that could mean an operating loss of about €700 million for the year.

Late May 2023 · first drop

Adidas sells part of the stock on its own website and app. It brings in about $437 million, around €400 million. That is about a quarter of the stock.

Aug to Sep 2023 · second drop

Another drop brings in about €350 million. In 2023 Yeezy sales reach about €750 million, with about €300 million of operating profit. Adidas drops the €500 million write-off.

2024 · the rest

More drops through the year bring in about €650 million and €200 million of profit. The last pair is sold at the end of 2024. Only damaged pairs and odd sizes nobody wants are written off.

Where the money went

About €1.4 billion of sales gave about €500 million of operating profit. Adidas set aside €260 million for charity, about half the profit. €60 million has been paid to groups that fight hate, and €200 million sits in a new Adidas foundation.

Chapter 2 · The options

Burn it, keep it, or sell it

In May 2023 the new CEO, Bjørn Gulden, said burning the shoes would not be a solution. Giving the shoes away was also ruled out, because free pairs would end up on resale sites. That left three real options.

✗ Biggest loss

Destroy the stock

✗Cash: a €500M write-off, no sales ~Brand: photos of burning shoes make news too ✗Waste: millions of new pairs gone
✗ Only delays it

Keep it in the warehouse

✗Cash: €1.2B stuck, plus storage bills ✓Brand: quiet for now ~Waste: shoes age and sizes run out
✓ What Adidas did

Sell it and give part away

✓Cash: about €500M of profit instead of a loss ~Brand: people ask if Adidas is profiting from Ye's name ✓Waste: almost every pair is worn

Resale prices for some Yeezy models more than doubled after production stopped. The demand was still there.

So for supply chain, the question is:

When stock you can't sell the normal way fills your warehouses, how fast do you sell it off?

Chapter 3 · You decide

Pick the speed

It's May 2023 and you have decided to sell. Selling fast means big discounts and cheap Yeezys everywhere at once. Selling slowly keeps prices up, but the shoes age and the story stays in the news. Each square is 1% of the stock.
