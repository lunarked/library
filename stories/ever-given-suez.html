Story: Ever Given blocks the Suez Canal
Decision story · Logistics disruption

One ship, sideways, and 422 ships waiting

In March 2021 the container ship Ever Given got stuck across the Suez Canal for six days. Ship captains had to choose between waiting and sailing around Africa. Then the delayed ships all reached Europe's ports at once. Here is what happened, what each choice cost, and what I would have done.

Chapter 1 · The jam

Tuesday, 23 March, 7:40 a.m.

The Suez Canal is a 193 km ditch through the desert that links Asia to Europe. About 12% of the world's trade goes through it, roughly 50 ships a day. Without it, a ship from Asia to Europe has to sail all the way around Africa.

Normal day

Ships enter in convoys from both ends. Much of the canal is a single lane, so ships pass in one direction at a time.

23 Mar · 7:40 a.m.

In a sandstorm with gusts over 64 km/h, the Ever Given, one of the world's biggest container ships and bound for Rotterdam, drifts into the bank. Its bow hits the east wall and its stern the west. The canal is shut.

25 Mar · Day 2

About 185 ships are waiting. Lloyd's List estimates the jam holds up $9.6 billion of trade a day: $5.1bn going west to Europe and $4.5bn going east.

26 Mar · Day 3

Salvors warn it could take weeks. Maersk, MSC and Hapag-Lloyd start sending some services around Africa, about 10 extra days. In total, 17 Asia–Europe and Asia–US ships turn around.

29 Mar · Day 6

Tugs, dredgers and a spring tide pull the ship free. By now 422 ships are in the queue.

3 Apr · Day 11

Egypt runs extra convoys day and night. The last of the 422 ships pass through on 3 April. The jam is over. Europe's problems are just starting.

So for supply chain, the question is:

When a route closes and nobody knows for how long, when do you stop waiting?

Chapter 2 · Decision 1

Wait in line, or go around Africa?

Put yourself on 26 March, day 3. Your ship is on its way from Asia to Rotterdam. Salvors say it could be weeks. Going around the Cape of Good Hope is certain, but it adds about 3,400 nautical miles, 7 to 10 days from Asia. Waiting is free only if the canal opens soon. And once it opens, you still join the back of a queue of hundreds.

The answer depends on two things: where your ship is and how long you think the block will last. Try it.

Chapter 3 · The wave

Silence, then everyone at once

A ship takes about 11 days from the canal to Rotterdam. So a canal that is shut for six days doesn't just delay ships. It creates a hole in Europe's arrivals, and then a wave. About 60 ships bound for Rotterdam were in the queue.

Normal

On a normal day, about 7 ships arrive from the Suez route. Cranes, trucks, barges and trains are planned around that.

3 to 8 April · the hole

The ships that would have left the canal during the block never arrive. Cranes stand idle. Exports have no ship: SeaIntelligence expected export capacity from Europe to drop by almost 80%.

9 to 18 April · the wave

Egypt let ships out in growing groups, 12 to 15 hours apart, to soften the peak. It still arrives as a wave. Rotterdam expects traffic about 10% above normal every day for weeks and limits early export deliveries to keep space free.

Mid to late April · the stragglers

The ships that went around Africa arrive last, about 10 days late. HMM warned that "another spike will be apparent" when they land.

The ripple

Empty containers are stuck in the wrong place, and carriers need them back in Asia. Spot rates from Asia to North Europe were already about $7,500 per 40-ft box and reached about $13,000 by July. Covid demand drove most of that. Suez was one more push.

Chapter 4 · Decision 2

How do you land the wave?

You run a terminal in Rotterdam. About 48 extra ships are coming over ten days, on top of normal traffic. The terminal can push to about 9 ships a day. If ships pile up at anchor, the yard fills, cranes slow down, and it gets worse. Pick a plan.

The decisions that could have been made
  1. Before

    Plan for a closed route

    Know which orders ride through Suez. Hold extra stock of critical Asian parts. Keep a rail or air option on contract.

  2. During · 23 Mar – 3 Apr

    Triage the boxes

    Find your containers on stuck ships on day 1. Fly the critical few. Warn customers early. Book trucks for the wave first.

  3. After · 2021 on

    Fix the weak spot

    Egypt widened the southern stretch. Shippers: size buffers for a 2-week route closure, not just normal delays.

Afterwards, Egypt held the ship for over three months and first claimed $916 million, later lowered to about $550 million.
