Story: McDonald's E. coli onions (2024)
Decision story · Food safety, supplier recall

McDonald's pulled the Quarter Pounder in 1 in 5 US restaurants and traced the E. coli to one onion plant

In October 2024, people who ate Quarter Pounders got sick with E. coli. Nobody knew yet if it was the beef or the onions. McDonald's took the burger off the menu across 12 western states, found the onions within five days, and dropped the supplier. Here is how the trace worked, and how wide a pull should be when you don't know yet what's bad.

Chapter 1 · The outbreak

Tuesday, 22 October 2024

The US health agency, the CDC, announces an E. coli outbreak. 49 people are sick in 10 states. One older man in Colorado has died. Almost everyone ate at McDonald's, and most of them had a Quarter Pounder.

McDonald's has about 14,000 US restaurants. Watch where the burger goes dark.

12 Sep to 21 Oct

People fall ill over five weeks. Half the cases are in Colorado (26). Then Nebraska, Utah, Wyoming. Each dot is one sick person.

Tue 22 Oct · day 1

The same day, McDonald's pulls the Quarter Pounder in Colorado, Kansas, Utah and Wyoming, and in parts of eight more states. About 1 in 5 US restaurants. The other 4 in 5 keep selling it.

Sun 27 Oct · day 6

The beef is cleared. The burger comes back in the pulled states, but without slivered onions. By mid-November it is back on every menu.

Late Oct to Dec

The burger was only pulled in the West, but customers stayed away across the whole country. Visits to McDonald's fell about 9% across the US, and up to 33% in Colorado. The final count: 104 sick in 14 states, about a third in hospital. The CDC called it over on 3 December.

Chapter 2 · The trace-back

Finding the bad ingredient

To find the bad ingredient, investigators work backwards: from the sick person, to the meal, to each ingredient, to the plant that made it, to every restaurant it went to.

Step 1 · the meal

The sick people ate different things, but the Quarter Pounder keeps showing up. That narrows the search to one burger.

Step 2 · what's special about it

Two ingredients are used only on the Quarter Pounder: fresh beef patties and slivered onions. Both are suspects, so both come off the menu.

Fri 25 Oct · beef cleared

Colorado tests the beef patties from local restaurants. Every sample is negative. The beef is off the list.

The onions

The slivered onions came from one supplier: a Taylor Farms plant in Colorado Springs. It fed three distribution centers, and they fed about 900 restaurants.

Sun 27 Oct · supplier cut

McDonald's stops buying onions from that plant "indefinitely" and moves to another supplier. Taylor Farms recalls its yellow onions. Burger King, Taco Bell, Pizza Hut and KFC pull onions in some areas too.

Chapter 3 · How wide to pull

Three rings McDonald's could draw

On day 1 nobody knew yet if it was beef or onions. A pull that is too small leaves bad food on the grill. A pull that is too big shuts down restaurants with nothing wrong, and tells the whole country there's a problem.

✗ Too small on day 1~900

Only where the onions went

Smallest pull, but it bets on onions before the beef is cleared.
✓ What McDonald's did1 in 5

The whole region

Covers both suspects where the cases are. The rest of the country keeps selling.
✗ Too big14,000

Every US restaurant

Safest on paper. Shuts thousands of restaurants that never got either ingredient.

Chapter 5 · The decision

What I would have done

My call

Pull the burger across the region on day 1, make the pull smaller as each test comes back, and drop the supplier once the onions are confirmed.

My plan, day by day · each square is 100 restaurants
Day 1Tue 22 Oct
2,800 restaurants pulled

It could be the beef or the onions. Pull the burger in all 12 states with cases.

✓ Same as McDonald's
Day 4Fri 25 Oct
~900 restaurants pulled

The beef tests clean. Bring the burger back everywhere except the restaurants that got the onions.

+ My change: burger back 2 days earlier in 1,900 restaurants
Day 6Sun 27 Oct
0 restaurants pulled

The onion plant is dropped and a new supplier ships. The burger is back on every menu.

✓ Same as McDonald's

Bringing 1,900 restaurants back two days early saves about $2.3M in burger sales est. and shows customers that the beef is safe.

What I would change before and after
Before

Trace onions by batch

Know which batch went to which restaurant, so a pull can target batches instead of whole states.

Before

Approve a second supplier

Have a backup approved in each region for fresh produce, so a switch takes only a few days.

After

Help the hardest-hit owners

McDonald's gave $65M to franchise owners and spent $35M on marketing. I would aim it at Colorado first, where visits fell most.

The same call in hardware

When a batch of parts is bad, the question is whether to stop every product that uses the part or only the batches you can trace. Batch-level tracing is what makes a small pull safe.
