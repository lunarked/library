Story: Peloton overbuilds (2020 to 2022)
Decision story · Peloton, 2020 to 2022

Peloton built for a boom that ended

In 2020 everyone wanted a bike at home. Peloton added factories and stock to keep up. This is the question it faced, what it decided, and what happened.

Scroll to start
1 · The problem

Lockdowns sent demand far past what Peloton could make

In 2020 gyms closed and people bought bikes for home. Sales doubled in a year. Buyers waited weeks, some months. Peloton spent $100 million to fly bikes in from Asia, at about ten times the usual shipping cost.

2 · The question Peloton asked

Build for the boom, or for normal demand?

Peloton had to decide how many factories and how much stock to add, without knowing how long home workouts would last.

So for supply chain, the question is:

How much should you build for a demand spike that may not last?

3 · Why it mattered

Both mistakes were costly

Every month of waiting sent buyers to rivals. But a factory and a warehouse of bikes cannot be turned off when demand falls.

4 · What Peloton decided

Build for the boom

Peloton bought Precor to make bikes in the US and planned a $400 million plant in Ohio with 2,000 jobs. Its capacity was six times higher than a year earlier, and it planned $5.4 billion in sales for the year to June 2022.

5 · What happened

Gyms reopened and sales fell below the plan

In November 2021 Peloton cut its sales forecast by up to $1 billion. Its shares fell more than 30% in a day. The year ended at $3.6 billion.

5 · What happened

Peloton stopped the lines

In January 2022 it paused making bikes and treadmills so it could sell the stock it had. In February it cancelled the Ohio plant, cut 2,800 jobs, and its founder stepped down as CEO.

5 · What happened

A warehouse full of bikes

Stock grew to $1.4 billion in March 2022. Peloton wrote down unsold stock and paid suppliers to cancel parts it no longer needed.

5 · What happened

Peloton stopped making its own bikes

In mid 2022 it closed its own factory in Taiwan and gave all bike and treadmill making to outside makers.

The lesson

Own the capacity for demand that will last, and rent the rest

Steady demand can fill your own factory. A spike is better met with outside makers, overtime and longer waits, which stop when the spike ends. est.
