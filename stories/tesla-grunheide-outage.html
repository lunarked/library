Story: Tesla Grünheide power outage
Decision story · Supply chain risk

The power tower that stopped a gigafactory

In March 2024 a fire under one power line cut Tesla's Berlin plant off the grid for eight days. Here is what happened, what it cost, and what the plant could have done instead.

Chapter 1 · The night

Tuesday, 5 March, before dawn

Tesla's plant in Grünheide, east of Berlin, builds the Model Y. On a normal day about 1,000 cars leave the line. Like most factories, it gets its power from a single high-voltage line running across open fields.

Normal day

Power flows from the substation, over the power tower, into the plant. The same line also feeds villages nearby.

Around 5 a.m.

Someone sets fire to the power tower in a field near the plant. A far-left group called "Vulkangruppe" later claims the attack. The plant goes dark. So do tens of thousands of homes in Brandenburg and Berlin.

The backup fails

The plant has backup systems, but the fire cut the cables between the mast and the substation. Ventilation stops too. All 12,500 workers are sent home.

Chapter 2 · The cost

Eight days of nothing

A car plant doesn't pause cleanly. The paint shop has to be emptied and restarted. Parts keep arriving with nowhere to go. Every day dark is a day of cars that customers don't get.

Day 1 · Tue 5 Mar

About 1,000 cars are lost on the first day. The paint shop and line start costing money just to bring back.

Day 2 · Wed 6 Mar

Plant director André Thierig says the loss will be in the "high hundreds of millions" of euros. He doesn't expect a restart that week.

Day 7 · Mon 11 Mar

The grid operator works in three shifts. Power comes back at 9:30 p.m. Repairing the power tower alone costs over €1M.

Day 9 · Wed 13 Mar

The line runs again. In total about 8,000 cars were not built. The loss is later estimated at about €400M. In April, Tesla names the attack as one reason its Q1 output fell.

Chapter 3 · Why it happened

A €5 billion plant, one line in a field

The arson was the trigger. The weakness was older. The plant depended on one feed, through one power tower that anyone could walk up to. The threat was known: in 2021 a left-wing group claimed an arson attack on cables at the plant's construction site.

The fire was the trigger.

The weak spot was the design.

So for supply chain, the question is:

What is it worth to never depend on one power tower again?

The weak spot

One line, one power tower. Nothing else could keep the plant running. The plant had four ways to protect itself.

Option 0 · Do nothing

Accept the risk and rely on insurance. It costs nothing up front. Insurance may pay back part of the money, but it builds no cars. Customers still wait.

Option 1 · Diesel gensets

About 20 MW for ventilation, the paint shop and IT. Up in weeks, ~€2M a year. They don't run the line, but the plant shuts down safely and restarts about a day sooner. Saves ~€57M of the €400M.

Option 2 · Battery storage

About 400 MWh would run the whole plant for ~4 hours, at ~€4M a year after grid income. Great for short dips. In an 8-day cut it is empty by breakfast. Saves ~€16M.

Option 3 · A second grid feed

A new line on a separate route, from a different substation. ~€50M one-off, shared with the grid operator, so ~€3M a year. It takes 2 to 4 years to permit and build. One burnt power tower no longer stops the plant. Saves ~€360M.

Chapter 4 · Running the numbers

It depends on how often it happens

Protection costs money every year. An outage costs money only when it happens. So each option is worth it if the loss it saves, times the chance per year, beats its yearly cost. Try it yourself.

Chapter 5 · The decision

What I would have done

My call

Build a second, independent grid feed, and bridge the years it takes with backup for critical systems.

It covers the real eventThe feed saves ~€360M of the €400M. No other option saves more than €57M in an 8-day cut.
It is cheap next to the risk~€3M a year against ~€16M of expected loss a year at a 1-in-25 risk. It still pays off at 1 in 120 years.
It has a gap, so close itA new line takes years. Gensets go in within weeks, give a safe shutdown, and stay as a second layer.
The decisions that could have been made
Before · 2020

Plan two feeds

The plant was built from scratch. Adding a second route at the design stage is far cheaper than retrofitting one later.
During · March 2024

Run a playbook

Pause supplier call-offs, hold inbound trucks, tell customers new delivery dates on day 1. These need no grid, only preparation.
After · 2024 on

Fix the weak spot

Second feed, critical-load backup, and power tower monitoring with the grid operator. I found no public report of a new independent feed.
