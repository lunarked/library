Story: Zara near-shoring (1990s)
Decision story · Zara, 1990s

Why Zara kept its factories close to home

Most clothing brands moved their sewing to Asia to save money. Zara did not. This is the problem it faced, the question it asked, what it decided, and what happened.

Scroll to start
1 · The problem

Fashion changes faster than clothes can be made

In the 1990s, a brand had to guess months ahead what people would want to wear. It ordered a whole season from Asia and shipped it by sea. A wrong guess ended up on sale or never sold.

2 · The question Zara asked

Make clothes where it is cheapest, or where it is fastest?

Zara was growing across Europe. Its founder, Amancio Ortega, had to decide where its clothes would be made.

So for supply chain, the question is:

Is it worth paying more per item to get clothes into stores faster?

3 · Why speed mattered

A wrong guess cost more than higher wages

Typical brands sold 30 to 40% of their clothes at a discount, and about a fifth never sold at all. For Zara, the bigger cost was at the end of the season, not in the factory.

4 · What Zara decided

Keep trend items close and send basics far

Zara cut fabric in its own plants in Galicia and had small workshops nearby sew it. Basics stayed in cheap Asian factories. A spokesman later said that to cut lead time, Zara had to produce closer to home, even if it was not cheaper.

5 · What happened

New designs reached stores in about two weeks

Zara sent small batches, watched what sold and made more of the winners. It could change 40 to 50% of its orders after the season started. Most brands could change about 20%.

5 · What happened

Fewer clothes on sale, fewer left over

Zara sold only 15 to 20% of its clothes on sale, and less than 10% were never sold. Out of 100 items, more sold at full price. est.

5 · What happened

Shoppers kept coming back

The stores changed every few days, so people visited more often and bought before items were gone.

The lesson

Paying more per item made sense for clothes that are hard to predict

Speed cut the much bigger cost of guessing wrong. Zara still pays more for labor, and keeps cheap Asian production for items where a guess is easy.
