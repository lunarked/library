Why plan backwards

Start from the date you promisedGo-live is fixed by the customer contract. Commissioning, IT install, fit-out, cooling and power each take time, so every phase has a start date you can count back to.

Each phase gates equipmentSwitchgear is useless before the power room exists, and servers can't land before the white space is clean. So each item is needed on site the day its phase starts, and no earlier, or it sits in a paid warehouse.

Order-by = need date − drop − leadDrop time covers shipping, customs, warehouse and the final move to site. Lead time is the factory queue. The item with the least slack is the critical path. In most real builds it is the power equipment, not the servers.

Two levers when it's redPay to cut lead time (reserve factory slots, buy from stock, expedite), or move the go-live date. The banner shows both. A third lever, splitting the phases so they overlap, is what you would negotiate with the construction team.

All lead times, drop times and phase lengths are illustrative examples, set in the range public industry reporting gave for 2024–2026. Real values depend on rating, vendor and region. "Reserve factory slots" cuts lead time by 30% on items over 30 weeks, usually paid for with a deposit.
